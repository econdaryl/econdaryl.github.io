Reshaping Economic Geography:

Evidence from NYC Congestion Pricing

Daryl Larsen

2025-08-15

Motivation: Even Good Economics Isn’t a Free Lunch

Implementing the planned congestion pricing system risks too many unintended consequences for New Yorkers at this time.

— Governor Kathy Hochul, June 5, 2024

The Tradeoff:

  • Congestion pricing is a powerful tool for saving time and reducing pollution
  • But it likely involves fewer visits to an area, potentially harming businesses
  • Cordon pricing in particular can reshape the economic geography of a city

Background: NYC Congestion Pricing

Two Main Goals:

  1. Reduce congestion (noise, traffic speed, pollution)
  2. Raise revenue for transit ($1B annually)

Implementation:

  • 1,400 license plate scanners
  • Discounts for low-income residents
  • Exemptions for buses, government vehicles, disabled

Did It Work? Early Evidence Says Yes

Traffic Volume: Down 7.5%

Average Speed: Up 9%

Revenue: On track for $500M in 2025

But what about businesses?

  • Fewer customers driving in
  • But some evidence of more pedestrians and cyclists
  • Spatial redistribution of economic activity

Identification Strategy: Why This Policy Matters

Natural Experiment Setup:

  • Policy implemented January 5, 2025 (after 6-month delay)
  • Sharp geographic boundary (Congestion Relief Zone)
  • Exogenous timing relative to business decisions

Key Challenge: Business Heterogeneity - Not all businesses equally exposed to vehicle-dependent customers - Location within zone ≠ exposure to congestion pricing

Solution: Novel Exposure Measure \[Exposure_i = \sum_j \frac{V_{ij}}{V_i} \times \mathbb{I}(\text{tract } j \notin CRZ) \times \pi_{ij}\]

Where \(V_{ij}\) = visitors from tract \(j\), \(\pi_{ij}\) = vehicle mode share

Literature: Limited Evidence on Business Effects

Congestion Pricing Studies:

  • London, Stockholm, Singapore show traffic reduction
  • Mixed findings on business impacts:
    • Daunfeldt et al. (2009): No effect on shopping malls
    • Quddus et al. (2007): 5-8% sales decline (1 store)
    • Tang (2021): 20% land value increase

Key Limitation: Small samples, limited methods

SafeGraph Spending: Interpretation

Spatial Pattern of Effects:

  1. Inside CPZ: Small negative effect on spending (-1.8%) vs larger effect on transactions (-2.6%)
    • Suggests people visit less but may spend slightly more per visit
  2. Adjacent Areas (0-15km): Positive spillover effects
    • Businesses just outside the zone benefit from diverted economic activity
    • Ring 2 (5-15km) shows largest gains: +6.8% spending, +7.0% transactions
  3. Economic Interpretation:
    • Net economic effect may be smaller than visit data alone suggests
    • Spatial redistribution of economic activity rather than pure loss
    • Some evidence for “quality over quantity” - fewer but potentially higher-value visits

Business Exposure to Congestion Pricing

Beyond Location: Measuring True Exposure

Key Insight: Location ≠ Exposure

Same Location, Different Exposure:

  • Tourist restaurant vs. local coffee shop
  • Both inside CRZ, vastly different customer bases
  • Traditional DiD misses this heterogeneity

Solution: Customer origin-based exposure measure

Exposure Formula: \[Exposure_i = \sum_j \frac{V_{ij}}{V_i} \times \mathbb{I}(j \notin CRZ) \times \pi_{ij}\]

Where:

  • \(V_{ij}\) = visitors from tract \(j\) to business \(i\)
  • \(\mathbb{I}(j \notin CRZ)\) = tract outside zone
  • \(\pi_{ij}\) = vehicle mode share for O-D pair

Transportation Mode Estimation

Challenge: How likely are customers to drive from tract \(j\) to business \(i\)?

Data Sources:

  • NYMTC Regional Household Travel Survey (2019): 12K+ NYC metro respondents
  • NHTS 2022: National benchmarks for calibration
  • Complete 3.5M tract-to-tract mode share matrix

Mode Categories:

  • Vehicle: Car, taxi (subject to congestion charge)
  • Transit: Bus, rail, subway
  • Active: Walk, bike
  • Other: Mixed modes

Conclusion & Policy Implications

Key Findings

1. Revenue Effects: 3-4% decline in business spending inside CRZ

2. Spatial Spillovers: Effects extend beyond zone boundaries to adjacent areas

3. Business Heterogeneity: Exposure matters more than location alone

Policy Implications

For Policymakers:

  • Consider customer-base heterogeneity in impact assessment
  • Geographic spillovers require regional coordination
  • Revenue redistribution mechanisms may be warranted

For Urban Economics:

  • Traditional spatial methods may miss heterogeneity
  • Need for exposure-based treatment analysis
  • Network effects in urban policy evaluation
